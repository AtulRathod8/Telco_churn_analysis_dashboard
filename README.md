# 📊 Telecom Customer Churn Analysis | Power BI Dashboard

> An interactive Power BI dashboard for analyzing telecom customer churn, customer demographics, tenure, contracts, services, payment methods, and billing behavior to identify customer segments associated with churn and support data-driven retention strategies.

---

## 📌 Project Overview

Customer churn is a major business challenge in the telecommunications industry. Losing customers can directly affect recurring revenue, customer lifetime value, and long-term business growth.

This project analyzes telecom customer data using **Microsoft Power BI** to understand customer churn patterns and identify the characteristics of customers who are more likely to leave the company.

The dashboard provides an interactive business intelligence solution that allows users to compare churned and retained customers across multiple dimensions, including:

* Customer demographics
* Gender
* Senior citizen status
* Partner status
* Customer tenure
* Contract type
* Internet service
* Phone service
* Online security
* Technical support
* Payment method
* Monthly charges
* Total charges

The objective is not only to visualize churn but also to transform customer data into **actionable business insights for customer retention**.

---

## 🎯 Business Problem

Telecom companies need to understand:

* Why customers leave
* Which customer segments have higher churn
* Which contracts are associated with higher customer attrition
* Whether customer tenure influences churn
* Whether service adoption is related to churn
* Whether billing and payment behavior differs between customer groups
* Which customer segments should receive greater retention attention

This dashboard addresses these questions through interactive visual analysis.

---

## 🎯 Project Objectives

The primary objectives of this project are:

1. Analyze overall customer churn behavior.
2. Compare churned and existing customers.
3. Identify customer segments associated with higher churn.
4. Analyze churn across different tenure groups.
5. Understand the relationship between contract type and churn.
6. Analyze churn across different internet services.

8. Analyze the impact of additional services such as Online Security and Tech Support.

# 📊 Key KPIs

The dashboard focuses on several important customer and churn KPIs.

| KPI                           | Business Purpose                                                      |
| ----------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Total Customers**           | Measures the overall customer base                                    |
| **Churned Customers**         | Measures the number of customers who have left                        |
| **Churn Rate**                | Measures the proportion of customers lost                             |
| **Average Monthly Charges**   | Measures average recurring customer billing                           |
| **Average Total Charges**     | Indicates cumulative customer billing                                 |
| **Customer Tenure**           | Measures customer relationship duration                               |
| **Churn by Contract**         | Identifies contract types associated with churn                       |
| **Churn by Internet Service** | Compares churn across internet service categories                     |
| **Churn by Payment Method**   | Identifies payment-method-related churn patterns                      |
| **Churn by Tenure Group**     | Highlights customer lifecycle stages associated with churn            |
| **Churn by Support Services** | Examines the relationship between support services and churn          |
| **Churn by Online Security**  | Examines the relationship between security-service adoption and churn |

### ⭐ Most Important KPIs for Management

For an executive-level view, the most important KPIs are:

**1. Total Customers**

Shows the size of the customer base being analyzed.

**2. Churned Customers**

Shows the absolute number of customers lost.

**3. Churn Rate**

The most important retention KPI because it normalizes churn against the total customer base.

**4. Average Monthly Charges**

Helps understand the recurring revenue characteristics of customers.

**5. Average Total Charges**

Provides an indication of cumulative customer value.

# 🔍 Key Analytical Insights

The dashboard is designed to highlight several important churn patterns.

### 1. Contract Type Is a Critical Churn Dimension

Contract type provides an important segmentation of customer churn.

Customers with shorter contractual commitments can be analyzed separately from customers with longer commitments.

**Business implication:**

The business can investigate targeted retention and contract-renewal strategies for customers approaching renewal or operating on flexible contracts.

---

### 2. Tenure Helps Identify Customer Lifecycle Risk

Tenure analysis allows the business to identify churn patterns across different stages of the customer lifecycle.

**Business implication:**

Customer retention programs can be designed differently for:

* Newly acquired customers
* Medium-tenure customers
* Long-term customers

---

### 3. Internet Service Requires Segment-Level Analysis

Different internet service categories show different customer behavior patterns.

**Business implication:**

The company can investigate service quality, pricing, customer experience, and support issues within individual internet-service segments rather than treating the entire customer base identically.

---

### 4. Billing Behavior Can Reveal Revenue Risk

Monthly and total charges provide a financial perspective on customer churn.

**Business implication:**

Customers with higher recurring charges may represent greater revenue exposure when they churn, making customer-level revenue segmentation useful for retention planning.

---

### 5. Support Services Can Be Used for Customer Segmentation

Technical Support and Online Security are analyzed as additional customer services.

**Business implication:**

The business can investigate whether customers without these services have different churn patterns and whether service adoption could become part of customer engagement strategies.

---

### 6. Payment Method Provides Another Retention Segment

Payment methods allow the business to investigate whether billing preferences are associated with customer churn.

**Business implication:**

Payment experience and billing communication can be evaluated as part of the overall customer experience.

---

### 7. Demographic Variables Provide Additional Context

Gender, senior citizen status, and partner status provide additional customer segmentation.

These variables should be used primarily for **understanding customer segments**, rather than assuming that demographics alone cause churn.

# 🧮 DAX & Analytical Calculations

The project demonstrates Power BI analytical capabilities through KPI calculations and aggregations.

Key analytical calculations include:

* Customer counts
* Churn counts
* Average Monthly Charges
* Average Total Charges
* Customer segmentation
* Tenure grouping
* Churn comparisons

Example conceptual KPI:

```DAX
Churn Rate =
DIVIDE(
    [Churned Customers],
    [Total Customers],
    0
)
```

Example customer count:

```DAX
Total Customers =
COUNTROWS('Telco-Churn-Analysis')
```

Example churned customer count:

```DAX
Churned Customers =
CALCULATE(
    COUNTROWS('Telco-Churn-Analysis'),
    'Telco-Churn-Analysis'[Churn] = "Yes"
)
```

> Note: These example measures are included to document the analytical approach. The exact DAX implementation in the PBIX may differ.

The project uses a telecom customer churn dataset containing customer demographics, account information, service usage, billing information, and churn status.

The commonly used Telco Customer Churn dataset contains **7,043 customer records and 21 fields**, with `Churn` as the target variable. IBM's documentation describes its Telco churn sample as fictional telecom customer data covering factors such as demographics, services, monthly charges and churn.
